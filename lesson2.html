LESSON 02 · BEGINNER

Understand the Timeline, Import, Trim & Split Clips

A timeline is where your video comes together. Learn to import media, arrange clips, remove unwanted moments, and make a clean first cut. These core ideas work across CapCut, Filmora, DaVinci Resolve, and most editing apps; button names can vary.

What You Will Learn

  • Recognize the media bin, preview monitor, timeline, and playback controls.
  • Import video and audio files.
  • Place clips in order and move them along the timeline.
  • Trim clip edges and split a clip at the playhead.
  • Organize and save a simple editing project.

Step-by-Step Tutorial

  1. Create or open a project. Choose an aspect ratio and frame rate suitable for your video. Default settings are fine for this practice if you are unsure.
  2. Import footage. Use Import, Add Media, or the equivalent. Select three short clips and one music or sound file.
  3. Identify the workspace. Locate the media bin/library, preview monitor, timeline, playhead, and playback controls. The media bin stores source files; the timeline defines the edit.
  4. Arrange the clips. Drag your clips onto the main video track in a logical order. Play the sequence to check its flow.
  5. Trim the beginning or end. Drag a clip edge inward to remove unwanted time. Trimming changes the visible duration without necessarily dividing the clip.
  6. Split at the playhead. Move the playhead inside a clip and use Split, Blade, or the scissors command. Check the cut before deleting an unwanted segment.
  7. Review and save. Play from the beginning, check for gaps or abrupt cuts, and save the project. Export a short test if possible.

Key Terms

TimelineTracks that define when video and audio play.
PlayheadThe marker showing the current time in your edit.
TrimAdjust a clip’s in-point or out-point to change its visible length.
SplitDivide one clip into separate segments at a chosen point.
Pro tip: Keep original footage untouched and save the editable project separately. Preview small edits often. A clean cut is usually more effective than lots of transitions.

Your Practical Challenge

Create a 20–30 second rough cut using three clips. Arrange them in sequence, trim at least one clip, split and remove one unwanted moment, then save the project.
